# grave-robber-sword-sheet.png

**What it is.** The Grave Robber (id 49) five ways, walking. Left to right: as shipped; without the
sheathed sword; holding the proposed backpack; holding the backpack with the sheathed sword gone;
and with the sword taken out of the pouch and put in the hand.
`grave-robber-sword/candidates-manifest.txt` names every tile.

**The question it asks.** Whether the sword stays. Id 49's own line in `docs/roster.md` calls it a
lie — the Grave Robber never attacks, and it walks the corridor wearing a blade it will not draw.
Nothing here picks one.

**A finding this sheet made on its own: tiles three and four are byte-identical.** With the
backpack in the hand, the pack covers the whole front of the body from the camera the game uses,
so the pouch sword cannot be seen either way. If the backpack is taken, the sword question does not
need answering; it only exists for a Grave Robber whose hands stay empty. That is a measurement off
the render, not a reading of it — `cmp` says the two PNGs are the same file.

**Why this needed a change to the set-file reader.** Both of the Hoarder's hands are empty in the
shipped binding: the blade sits in a child of the body mesh called `Hoarder_FrontPouch_Sword`, at
the belt. "Without it" cannot be asked by naming one fewer prop, and the pack ships no second body
with the pouch empty. So the model field grew a `!Node` suffix — `Hoarder.fbx!Hoarder_FrontPouch_Sword`
— which hides the named children for the render and touches nothing on disk. `CandidateSet` checks
every such name against the model before anything draws, so a misspelling is a fault listing what
the body does carry, rather than a picture that still has the sword in it.

**Rendered from.** `docs/roster-grave-robber-sword.txt`, through the game's own `CreepView`.

```powershell
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-grave-robber-sword.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/grave-robber-sword
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-grave-robber-sword.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/grave-robber-sword-1x -Width 28
```

**A still cannot answer "which animation", and this sheet is a still.** Posed at the strike, a chop
and a diagonal slice are two pictures of a body holding a hammer; what tells them apart is the path
the hammer took to get there, and that is in no single frame. So the same set is also drawn as
filmstrips -- twelve frames across each clip, laid left to right, with `strip-index.txt` beside them
giving each clip's length in seconds:

```powershell
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-grave-robber-sword.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/grave-robber-sword -Strip 12 -Width 220
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-grave-robber-sword.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/grave-robber-sword-1x -Strip 12 -Width 28
```

The camera is framed once across every phase rather than per frame, so the body moves in the strip
and the world does not. The strips are not committed -- they are regenerated by whoever wants to
look, like every other candidate render here.

**Two framings, and the small one decides.** The `-Width 28` pass is gameplay size — about the 24
pixels a body gets at 1600×900, measured off `docs/frames/played-run/a-slow-nobody-can-see.png`.
The sword is a small thing at the belt, so whether removing it is visible at all is exactly the
kind of question the big picture answers wrongly.
