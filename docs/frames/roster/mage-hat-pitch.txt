# mage-hat-pitch-20.png, mage-hat-pitch-35-shipped.png, mage-hat-pitch-55.png

**What they are.** Tick 1546 of the recorded match — a Mage casting — at three camera pitches. The
tick, the distance and the width are the committed `docs/frames/match-tick-1546.png`'s: `-Ticks
1546 -Distance 22 -Width 1600`. The only thing that moves is how far the camera looks down.
`35.264390` is `SceneFraming.CameraDefaultPitchDegrees`, the pitch the game ships at.

**The question they ask.** Whether the Mage's silhouette should read better than it does. The
flash sits on the head of the staff, which the Mage raises beside its own head, and the hat covers
the staff at the pitch this camera is fixed at — so the frame shows a flash and a tracer at the
right place rather than a staff with a light on the end of it. That the anchor is on the staff and
not on a height above the root is asserted in `ImportedArtTests`. Nothing here picks a pitch.

**What the sweep says, and it is a direction rather than an answer.** Seven pitches were rendered —
20, 25, 30, the shipped 35.264390, 45, 55 and 65. Looking down *more* makes it strictly worse: at
55 the Mage is a purple disc with a body under it, and at 65 more so. Looking down *less* is the
only direction that helps, and it costs the board: at 20 the Mage reads as a mage — face, robe,
book, and the flash clear of the brim — and the far half of the corridor has gone behind the near
rim, so the whole 51-hex board no longer fits the frame. **The staff head is still under the hat at
20.** So a pitch alone does not fix the thing the frame was taken about; it trades a legible tower
for an illegible board and gets partway.

**Drawn from content `F3896E6D53F411C4`**, which is what this repository holds — the same digest
the committed `docs/frames/match-tick-1546.png` carries, so these three and it are pictures of the
same match. The capture writes that record itself, into a `rendered-from.txt` beside the PNGs it
draws; it is quoted here because the three kept frames were copied out of seven working directories
that are not committed, and a provenance file this document wrote by hand would be a claim nothing
measured.

**Three committed, seven rendered.** The bracket and the baseline. The other four come back from:

```powershell
foreach ($p in @('20','25','30','35.264390','45','55','65')) {
    ./tools/capture-match-frames.ps1 -Ticks "1546" -Distance 22 -Width 1600 -Pitch $p `
        -OutDir "docs/frames/roster/mage-hat-pitch-$p"
}
```

`-Pitch` is new, and unlike `-Yaw` and `-Distance` it is only passed to the editor when it is
asked for: a pitch of zero is a camera looking level at the horizon, which is not a candidate
anybody wants, so an unset `-Pitch` leaves `SceneFraming.CameraDefaultPitchDegrees` to answer.

**Two of the three candidates on the ticket are not here, and why.**

- **The hatless Mage: the pack ships none.** `Assets/Art/Characters/` holds one `Mage.fbx` and the
  Adventurers pack ships `mage_texture.png` and `mage_texture_alt_A.png` but no second body. An
  atlas cannot take a hat off. So the candidate does not exist to be rendered, and swapping to
  another character's body is a different question from this one.
- **A different anchor could not be rendered under this ticket's own constraint.** The effect
  anchors are baked into `MatchArt.asset` by `MatchSceneBuilder` at scene-build time, so drawing
  the flash anywhere but the staff tip means rebinding that asset — and this ticket renders
  candidates without changing `roster.md`, `MatchArt.asset` or any content file. Moving the anchor
  is also the decision itself rather than a picture of it. It wants either a batch-argument
  override on `MatchFrameCapture` or a ticket of its own; the pitch sheet does not settle it,
  because the pitch sweep shows a pitch alone will not.
