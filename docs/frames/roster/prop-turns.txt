# prop-turns-sheet.png

**What it is.** The Witch's broom (id 43) and the Necromancer's scythe (id 38) at five turns each,
walking. Row one is the broom, row two the scythe; in each, left to right: flat as bound today,
`@0,0,-90`, `@0,0,90`, `@0,0,180`, `@0,0,-45`. `prop-turns/candidates-manifest.txt` names every
tile.

**The question it asks.** Which turn each prop hangs at. Nothing here picks one.

**Why five and not one.** Both props lie flat in the fist, because neither carries a turn in its
binding and the pack authors them along the shaft. `docs/roster-creep-bodies-rest.txt` already put
the upright `@0,0,-90` up — the number the Mage's and the Druid's staffs use — as one candidate
each. One candidate is a proposal, not a choice. A weapon parented to a hand turns with the arm, so
a turn is only exactly right in one pose, and `-90` and `+90` differ by which end of the prop ends
up above the fist: a broom bristles-up is a different object from a broom bristles-down, and
neither is readable off the number.

**Rendered from.** `docs/roster-prop-turns.txt`, through the game's own `CreepView`, posed in
`Walking_A` — which is what both rows are actually drawn in, since neither swings anything. A turn
that reads in an idle and buries the shaft in the body mid-stride is the failure this sheet is
looking for.

```powershell
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-prop-turns.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/prop-turns
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-prop-turns.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/prop-turns-1x -Width 28
```

**A still cannot answer "which animation", and this sheet is a still.** Posed at the strike, a chop
and a diagonal slice are two pictures of a body holding a hammer; what tells them apart is the path
the hammer took to get there, and that is in no single frame. So the same set is also drawn as
filmstrips -- twelve frames across each clip, laid left to right, with `strip-index.txt` beside them
giving each clip's length in seconds:

```powershell
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-prop-turns.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/prop-turns -Strip 12 -Width 220
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-prop-turns.txt `
    -OutDir docs/frames/roster/prop-turns-1x -Strip 12 -Width 28
```

The camera is framed once across every phase rather than per frame, so the body moves in the strip
and the world does not. The strips are not committed -- they are regenerated by whoever wants to
look, like every other candidate render here.

**Two framings, and the small one decides.** The sheet is the magnification. The `-Width 28` pass
is gameplay size — a body is about 24 pixels tall at 1600×900 in the built player, measured off
`docs/frames/played-run/a-slow-nobody-can-see.png` — and is what says whether a turn is a thing a
player can see or a thing only a contact sheet can.
