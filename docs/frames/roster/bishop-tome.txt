# bishop-tome-sheet.png and bishop-tome-played-sheet.png

**What they are.** Four tiles: the Bishop as he ships with the mace and no tome, the tome in his off
hand, the tome standing on the tile beside him, and the two props swapped. Drawn through the real
`TowerView` by `tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-bishop-tome.txt`, once at
`-Width 700` and once at `-Width 28`. Issue #281.

**The question.** `docs/roster.md`'s tier-2 Cleric line names `Cleric_Mace` and never says where
`Cleric_Tome` goes. A hand holds one thing, so the mace took the hand the tome was in — the way the
Blessing's book takes the Templar's hammer's — and the visible consequence is that **a hitscan magic
bolt now leaves the head of a blunt weapon**, because the anchor follows the prop.

**The fourth candidate is a frame and not a tile.** "The anchor moved off the mace" cannot be shown on a
sheet, because a sheet is a still of a posed character and carries no bolt. It is
`docs/frames/rung-candidates/bishop-tome-off-hand-anchored.txt`, drawn through the real match.

**Swapping the hands does not move the bolt, and it reads as though it would.** The anchor names
`Cleric_Mace`'s **tip**, and an anchor follows its prop rather than its hand — so with the mace in the
off fist the bolt leaves the off-hand mace's head, which is the same complaint from the other side of
the body. `bishop-mace-off-hand.txt` carries an explicit `anchor` line for exactly that reason.

**A bolt lives four ticks, and finding that took the whole instrument.** The two candidates that differ
*only* in where the bolt leaves from were rendered at every tick from 311 to 326: they are
**pixel-identical from 315 onward** and differ at 311, 312, 313 and 314. The first four ticks tried —
300, 320, 340 and 700 — produced two candidate sets that were byte-identical to each other, which would
have been a picture answering a question nobody asked. **Tick 700 on this defense is an empty corridor**:
five towers that all reach four hexes clear the wave by about tick 420.

**What the anchor is worth at the size the game is played at: 225 pixels.** Moving the bolt from the mace
head to the tome, at a fixed 1600×900 camera, against the same board:

| tick | pixels moved | of a 1,440,000-pixel frame |
|---|---|---|
| 311 | 225 | 0.016% |
| 312 | 120 | 0.008% |
| 313 | 91 | 0.006% |
| 314 | 19 | 0.001% |

And every one of the four candidates moves **at most 871 pixels — 0.06% of the frame**. Issue #280 put
anything crossing the air at 0.004–0.030%; **this whole question sits in that band.** So the head-of-a-mace
complaint is real and it is not a complaint a player can see at 1600×900. It is decided at magnification,
on the sheet, or it is decided as a matter of what the record should say rather than what the screen shows.

**Two things the eye found on the sheet.** The shipped mace is held low against the body and reads as
part of the silhouette, while the off-hand mace reads plainly as a mace — so *"where does the tome go"*
may be the smaller half of the question and *"is the mace visible at all"* the larger. And the tome on
the ground lies flat and dark, where the Consecration's font and the Blessing's statue both stand up.

**Regenerate:**

```powershell
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-bishop-tome.txt -Width 700
./tools/capture-armed-roster.ps1 -SetFile docs/roster-bishop-tome.txt -Width 28
./tools/capture-rung-candidates.ps1 -Only bishop-tome-off-hand,bishop-tome-off-hand-anchored,bishop-tome-beside,bishop-mace-off-hand
```

**It draws no board, no roster row and no price**, which is why `check-docs.ps1` exempts it.
