Sixteen ways a tower can become something else

A catalogue of upgrade topologies, each graded against the two things that actually constrain the answer: a run that is only ten rounds long, and a character pack of fifty-seven models that share one rig.

What this is, and is not

This does not choose. It synthesises the depth research's eleven-mechanism taxonomy and the KayKit roster inventory into concrete upgrade schemes you can hold against the packs. Nothing here is an art decision — every model named is an option, not a pick.

Two provenance notes. The roster inventory is on an unmerged branch (worktree-kaykit-character-roster) and its role readings are inferred from publisher renders, not from files on this machine — client/Assets/Art/Characters/ holds exactly two FBX today. And the bundle itself is not yet bought; #56 is still open on the licence check.

What is already decided

The filter

Six settled decisions kill most of the design space before any of it gets interesting. Worth stating together, because they are scattered across the vision and they interact.

DecisionWhat it does to upgrades
A run is ten wavesTen build phases, total. A five-tier ladder eats half the run on one tower. Two or three tiers is the honest ceiling, and every scheme below is costed against ten.
Anchors open offense, never defenseThe pick menu at waves 3/6/9 belongs to the creeps. So the upgrade graph is the defense's entire tech axis — there is no second one. That raises the bar on it considerably.
One purse, and interest is 10% a waveEvery upgrade competes with compounding. An upgrade is not just "is this stronger" — it is "is this stronger than 10% and a wave slot". Timing is free depth here, and it comes attached to whatever you pick.
Nothing persistsThe whole upgrade graph is available on run one. No unlock ramp, so the graph must be legible cold — which is the single strongest argument for a scheme whose leaves are named archetypes a stranger recognises.
The board is a maze, at several elevations, and elevation grants rangeRestored 6 August. Placement is live again, which puts HEIGHT, MOSAIC and AURA back on the table — all three were dead under the corridor.
Three attack types, three armour types, a Latin squareAn upgrade that changes attack type is the sharpest lever available — it moves shots-to-kill by 2× against the right armour — and it costs no new numbers, only a column value.
The one that surprised me

Because anchors were given to the offense, the defense has no pick axis at all — Element TD's eleven picks have no counterpart on your side of the board. Whatever you choose below is doing the job that, in the anchor game, two separate systems do. DRAFT is the cheapest way to give the defense a pick axis back without touching the anchor rule.

The catalogue

16 schemes, 5 families

Each card carries four grades. ROUNDS is how many of your ten build phases a full commitment eats. ART is what it needs beyond what KayKit already ships. SURFACE is the size of the thing the sweep harness has to price. READ is whether an opponent scouting your board can see it.

Family 1 — Vertical

The same tower, more of it. No identity choice; the decision is only when, and which tower gets the money.

LADDER

The tier ladder

Ranger → Ranger II → Ranger III. One line, no branches.

Rounds2–3 ArtRecolour only Surfacetypes × tiers ReadColour only

The floor. Every tower has two or three ranks bought with sauce; the whole build is a spending order. It works, it is unimpeachably legible, and the research names its exact failure: "a roster where you can eventually have everything has no build in it, only an order." With ten rounds and compounding interest that order is a real decision — but it is one decision repeated, not depth.

Worth keeping as the substrate underneath something else rather than as the answer. Element TD runs a ladder and a combination roster, and the interesting part is where they meet: tier-up is only available from a level-1 tower, so gold spent climbing is gold that cannot be converted into a higher tier.

What KayKit gives you

The Adventurers Extra tier ships three alternate textures per character. That is four visual ranks per class, free, with no geometry and no animation work. Add a removable helmet (the Knight and Barbarian_Large both ship one) and you have a fifth read.

TECH

Upgrade the type, not the tower

Buy "Ranger II" once; every Ranger on your board becomes one.

Rounds3–4 ArtRecolour only Surfacetypes × tiers ReadWhole board

The same ladder with the OWNER dial flipped, and it is much better than it sounds. Ten rounds cannot support per-instance upgrades on a board of a dozen towers — you would spend the run clicking. Per-type turns that into three or four large, legible commitments, each of which visibly changes everything you own of that kind at once.

Three things follow that no other vertical scheme gets. It is enormously juicy — the whole board reskins on a purchase, which is the cheapest big moment in the design. It makes going wide and going deep genuinely opposed, because a second class means a second ladder to fund. And it is the cheapest scheme in the catalogue for the harness, since a configuration lives on the unit type where the sweep already indexes.

What KayKit gives you

Identical to LADDER — the alt textures. But the payoff is bigger, because a palette swap that hits six towers simultaneously is a board-wide event rather than a detail.

RANK

Veterancy — towers earn their own upgrades

Kills feed experience; a tower ranks up during the run.

Rounds0 — it is free ArtColourways SurfaceA trajectory ReadRank pip

Bloons' heroes: level 1 every game, level 20 by the end of it, and run cash can buy a level early — money for tempo out of the one purse. It is simultaneously a depth mechanism and an accessibility mechanism, and it needs no persistence.

The hazard is well documented and it is about scale. Per-instance experience on every tower makes a stored ghost "a layout plus a biography" and makes the build helper unable to answer what a tower does. One levelling unit whose trajectory is fully determined by recorded inputs is a different object entirely — it replays exactly. So: one, or at most two, and never the whole board.

What KayKit gives you

Two models ship as four colourways of one mesh — The Ninja (Series 4) and The Magical Girls (Series 6). A four-rank line where the colour is the rank, with zero authoring. That is as close to a purpose-built veterancy asset as the bundle contains.

APEX

One capstone per run

A single tower can be raised past the ceiling, and it consumes others to get there.

Rounds4+ to reach ArtBoss models exist SurfaceOne per class ReadUnmissable

Bloons' Paragons, bounded to one. It gives a run a shape — the first six rounds are setup for the thing you build at wave eight — and it makes wave 10 a payoff round, which the anchor schedule already wants it to be.

The consumption half fails the legibility veto on its own terms: a tower that is gone cannot be read, and the resulting tower's power has no visible cause. Fixable, and the fix is cheap — the apex stands on a plinth built from what it ate, so the cost stays on the board.

What KayKit gives you

The boss-scale silhouettes are already there: Barbarian_Large, The Combat Mech (separate gatling and shield arms, so it upgrades visibly), The Skeleton Golem, The Monstrosity. And The Paladin ships a solid-gold statue of himself plus a plinth — a ready-made apex affordance nobody would think to commission.

Family 2 — Branching

The tower becomes a different thing, and you choose which. This is the family your question lives in.

FORK

The class fork

A Ranger reaches tier 2 and becomes a Marksman, a Druid or an Avian — pick one, forever.

Rounds2 ArtOne model per leaf Surfacebases × leaves ReadBest in class

This is the literal answer to "how does a Ranger become a Rogue or a Druid", and it is the scheme the asset pack is already shaped like. KayKit's Adventurers is not a set of models, it is a set of archetypes a stranger recognises without a tooltip — which is exactly the property a game with no unlock ramp needs, because the whole graph has to be legible on run one.

Five bases with two or three leaves each gives twelve to fifteen terminal towers. Every leaf is a real model that exists today, and because all fifty-seven share one rig, a class change is a mesh swap with zero animation work. The commitment is irreversible and public, which makes it a genuine read in the lobby: what someone forked into at round four tells you what their round-nine wave is being built to survive.

The trade-off, named. Twelve leaves is twelve authored towers with twelve sets of numbers, and the combination rule saves you nothing on that bill — it saves the player's memory, which is the bigger prize when nothing persists. The other cost is that the depth is between towers rather than inside one, so a player's whole decision does not fit in a single panel the way a Bloons crosspath menu does.

What KayKit gives you

Every leaf above is a shipped model. The bundle's real gift here is that Adventurers and Skeletons were built as mirrors — Knight/Warrior, Rogue/Rogue, Mage/Mage — so a tower line and its counterpart creep line already exist as matched silhouettes. If FEEDBACK ever gets built, that pairing is the art already done.

CROSSPATH

Three paths and a legality predicate

Bloons' scheme: spend down two paths, and the second one is capped.

Rounds4 on one tower ArtAttachment points Surface25 / tower ReadSilhouette

The best leverage in the genre: Bloons gets 64 configurations per tower out of 15 authored upgrades, and the leverage is in the predicate, not the upgrades — the rule that at most two paths may be touched and the second is capped discards 70% of the space, and the discarded 70% is the design.

Scaled to a ten-round run — three paths, three tiers, secondary capped at one — that is 25 legal configurations per tower from 9 authored upgrades, and a full commitment costs four rounds. Six towers gives 150 configurations from 54 upgrades. Nothing else in the catalogue comes close per authored thing.

The art veto is lifted, and this is the finding worth having

The depth research rules crosspathing out on art cost: making 64 configurations visually distinct is what forces Bloons to ship 52 unique models for the Wizard Monkey alone, and this project's pipeline is stock models plus palette swaps.

That arithmetic changes if the paths are attachment points rather than meshes. KayKit's Adventurers ships 25+ swappable weapons and accessories, characters have removable helmets, and everything is UV'd onto one 1024×1024 gradient atlas. Bind path A to the weapon socket, path B to the head socket and path C to the palette, and five options each gives 125 visually distinct configurations out of ten meshes and five PNGs, composed at runtime with no new geometry at all. The veto was on bespoke geometry per configuration; an attachment system never pays that bill.

The structural mismatch is the real objection, and it survives. A crosspath is a private optimisation; this game's signature is reading an opponent's composition. Depth that lives inside one tower is more accessible — the whole decision fits in one panel — and less interesting in a format built on scouting. It is also the cheapest thing here to bolt onto FORK later, since a legality predicate is free content and does not touch the roster.

What KayKit gives you

The Adventurers accessory set, the Fantasy Weapon pack in the same bundle, and the shared atlas. Also worth knowing: The Action Figure ships four swappable face plates and muzzle-flash meshes that are already authored — a working demonstration that the socket approach is how the pack is built.

MOSAIC

Neighbours decide what you can become

A Ranger beside a Mage may become a Druid. Beside a Rogue, a Marksman.

Rounds2 ArtLeaves + link VFX Surfaceroster² per pair ReadIf linked visibly

FORK with the BREADTH dial turned on: the leaf you may take is a function of what sits next to you. This makes where and what a single decision, which is precisely what the vision says elevation was restored to achieve — and it was dead under the corridor, so it is genuinely newly available.

The balance cost is bounded and small — a unit's value indexed by its neighbours is roster-squared per pair, low thousands, a rounding error against an overnight sweep. The presentation cost is real: an adjacency that is not drawn is an adjacency nobody can read, and it has to survive being scanned on two boards at once.

What KayKit gives you

No new models beyond FORK's leaves. The Medieval Hexagon pack — free, CC0, 200+ models on the same atlas — carries the tile vocabulary to mark a linked pair on the board itself rather than with a floating VFX, which is the cheaper and more readable answer.

Family 3 — Combinatorial

The tower is assembled from parts, and the parts are the vocabulary a player learns instead of a catalogue.

RECIPE

Ingredients — the Element TD scheme proper

One tower per subset of a small vocabulary. Six names predict fifty-six towers.

RoundsNeeds a pick axis ArtOne PNG per element Surface41–56 units ReadBest in class

The anchor game, taken whole. It is the only mechanism in the survey that is simultaneously a depth mechanism, an accessibility mechanism and a harness mechanism — a roster that compresses to six words is a tutorial and a content strategy in one lever. And its whole visual state is a material swap, which is the pipeline you have already bought.

Two problems, and the first is structural. Element TD's depth is not the combinations, it is the eleven metered picks — and this vision has already spent its pick menu on the offense. Recipes without a metered ingredient budget is just a big flat roster. You would need to either give the defense a pick axis back (see DRAFT) or accept that ingredients are bought with sauce, which removes the opportunity cost that makes the scheme work.

The second is a known failure with two studios' worth of evidence. A combinatorial roster pays breadth a superlinear return — the sixth ingredient does not add one tower, it adds five duals and ten triples — so the widest build is the natural power peak. Element TD 2 has been nerfing six-element builds since version 0.51; GemCraft cut its palette from nine gem types to six. The counter-measure is cheap and this project can afford it overnight: a sweep report of win rate binned by number of ingredients taken, on the first sweep and every sweep after.

What KayKit gives you

The gradient atlas is the entire pipeline: an element is one small PNG. Where it does not serve you is that a KayKit character reads as an archetype, not as an ingredient — a red Ranger reads as "a Ranger, in red", not as "Fire". Recipes want abstract or building towers, and the Medieval Hexagon kit's building_tower_A, _B, _catapult and archeryrange — each already shipping in four team colours — are a much better fit than the characters are.

FUSION

Two classes make a third

Ranger + Mage = Druid. Knight + Mage = Paladin. Rogue + Ranger = Marksman.

Rounds2–3 ArtModels exist SurfaceC(bases,2) ReadParents visible

RECIPE's combinatorics without needing a pick axis, because the ingredients are towers you already built. Five bases gives ten dual classes; six gives fifteen. The scarcity is board space and sauce, both of which already exist, so it costs no new currency and no new predicate.

What makes this land here rather than being a generic merge mechanic is that the KayKit roster is already a fantasy class lattice, so most fusions have a shipped model that visibly carries both parents:

Two costs. The consumption fails the legibility veto for the same reason APEX does — the parents are gone and the result has no visible cause — though here the mitigation is built in, because the result's silhouette is the evidence. And it needs a fixed-point check before anything ships: if a fusion's output can equal or exceed its better input at the same cost, the mechanic is an unbounded loop, and that is visible in the coefficients rather than in playtesting. GemCraft's "supergemming" was exactly this and it took a sequel to remove.

What KayKit gives you

Every result above ships today. The bundle also carries the transformation vocabulary for free — The Werewolf (lumberjack and wolf-headed), The Animatronic (intact and torn open) and The 4-GTN (clean and moss-overgrown) are each two models of one entity, which is the fusion moment authored as art rather than as a particle burst.

KIT

Buy the tower, then buy its weapon

The upgrade is a loadout slot, chosen at build time from a fixed catalogue.

Rounds1 per slot ArtShips in the pack Surfacetowers × items ReadWeapons only

Item layering is rated the worst balance surface in the survey and it fails the legibility veto hardest — an item equipped on a unit is invisible at this camera, and three items on thirty towers is thirty times invisible.

Two restrictions rescue it, and both are forced by the assets anyway. First, one slot, and the slot holds a weapon — a weapon is a silhouette, so it passes the veto that a generic trinket fails. Second, bought at build time from a fixed catalogue, never dropped, which keeps a stored ghost a layout rather than a biography. With one slot the surface is towers × items, which is small enough to enumerate.

What it buys that FORK does not: a reversible, cheap adjustment. If wave 9's anchor turns out to want pierce and you built impact, a weapon swap is the answer that does not cost you a tower.

What KayKit gives you

This is the scheme the pack is most literally built for. Adventurers ships 25+ swappable weapons and accessories; Skeletons ships 10+; the bundle includes a dedicated Fantasy Weapon pack. The Ranger's bow has an animated bowstring via blendshape. The Plant Warrior ships both a bud spear and a leaf bow — one model, two loadouts, already authored.

MERGE

Two of a kind make one better

Two Rangers on adjacent tiles combine into one Ranger II.

Rounds0 extra ArtFree SurfaceCheck the fixed point ReadOnly the result

Super Auto Pets' shape. It costs no new upgrade currency at all — the upgrade path is buying two of something — and it gives a wide early board a reason to become a narrow late one, which is a decent arc for a ten-round run.

It carries the same unbounded-loop hazard as FUSION and more sharply, because the inputs are identical: if merging two tier-1s produces something worth more than two tier-1s, merging is never wrong and the mechanic has no decision in it. The check is arithmetic, done once, before anything is built.

What KayKit gives you

Nothing new required — the alt textures again. The merge moment is the only art cost, and Kenney's free Particle Pack is already the identified source for that.

Family 4 — Positional and reversible

Upgrades that change where a tower is or what mode it is in, rather than what it is. All three were dead under the corridor and are alive again.

HEIGHT

The upgrade is elevation

Pay to raise a tower a level. Elevation grants range, so height is the stat.

Rounds1 ArtFree — tiles exist Surfacelevels ReadPhysically obvious

The maze reversal restored elevation and stated its purpose outright: height is "the reason a range upgrade pays off in a later round instead of being a flat stat", and it makes where and what into one decision instead of two. Nothing in the vision yet says a player can buy height — but if they can, the cheapest upgrade in the catalogue is also one of the most interesting, because its value depends entirely on the map this rotation generated.

It composes with everything. It needs no new stats, no new predicate and no new art. And it is the only upgrade whose worth a player can evaluate by looking at the board rather than by reading a tooltip — which is exactly what the legibility pillar is asking for.

What KayKit gives you

Medieval Hexagon, free and CC0, 200+ models on the shared atlas — tiles, plinths and structures. The Paladin's gold statue plinth again, and building_tower_A/_B/_base in four team colours the pack README describes as "for versus gameplay", which is the faction-colour requirement met by the same asset.

STANCE

A free toggle, not an upgrade

Each build phase, a tower may switch between two modes at no cost.

Rounds0 ArtPairs ship as pairs Surface×2 per tower ReadWhole model changes

Not an upgrade at all, and included because it solves a problem the anchor schedule creates. Anchors at 3, 6 and 9 make preparation a skill — but under any irreversible scheme, a player who prepared for the wrong thing at wave 6 is simply behind with no recourse. A free stance toggle is the recourse, and it costs nothing to author.

It also puts a decision in every build phase even when the player has no sauce, which matters in a ten-round run where two or three rounds are spent banking for interest.

What KayKit gives you

Three characters ship as two models of the same entity — The Werewolf, The Animatronic and The 4-GTN. That is a transformation mechanic authored as art, at no cost, and it is the sort of thing that is invisible until you go looking for it in an inventory.

AURA

Upgrade out of combat entirely

The tower stops shooting and starts making its neighbours better.

Rounds1–2 ArtNeeds a drawn link SurfaceBounded by degree ReadOnly if linked

The leaf that changes what a tower is for rather than how hard it hits. It is the sharpest possible expression of opportunity cost — you gave up a shooter — and it is the one leaf type that makes a layout matter as much as a roster.

It also opens the economy leaf, which is where the interest dial gets interesting: a Lorekeeper that pays sauce instead of damage is a bet on the back half of the run, priced against 10% compounding. That is Element TD's "Interest on the pick menu" move — the tech axis and the money axis meeting on one button — arriving through the upgrade graph instead.

What KayKit gives you

The support archetypes are all shipped and all unmistakable: The Paladin (winged helm, sunburst shield, and the statue), The Lorekeeper (crook staff, scrolls, a writing desk that matches him exactly), The Engineer (toolbelt), The Farmers (wheelbarrows and produce that doubles as a resource prop).

Family 5 — Economic

Schemes that change how the upgrade is acquired rather than what it is. Both compose with anything above.

DRAFT

Upgrades are offered, not bought

The public offering carries two or three upgrade cards, the same ones for everybody.

RoundsShares the offering ArtUI only SurfaceUnchanged ReadEveryone saw the menu

This is the cheapest way to give the defense back a pick axis. The anchor rule says anchors open offense — it does not say the ordinary offering may not carry a defensive option. Put upgrade cards in the same merged menu the creeps come from, and the tech choice and the offense choice become one decision over one list, which is the shape one purse was chosen for.

It also inherits the Mechabellum property the vision already banked: the opponent saw the same menu, so taking a thing because they also need it is a second-order decision that costs nothing to author. And a shared vocabulary is what makes a send a read rather than a guess.

The cost is a genuine tension with the anchor rule's reasoning. Anchors open offense because preparation has to have something to prepare against — an anchor that handed you a better tower would be a gift rather than a threat. Ordinary rounds are not anchor rounds, so this does not break the rule, but it does mean the offering is now doing two jobs. Whether that dilutes it is a sweep question about the ratio, not an argument.

What KayKit gives you

Nothing — this is interface work, and the interface is already named as the hardest visual problem in the design.

FEEDBACK

The upgrade unlocks a creep

Forking your Ranger into a Druid opens the nature creeps you may send.

Rounds0 extra ArtMirrors already exist SurfaceBoth halves at once ReadVery

The vision's own stated direction: "a tower of a given type unlocks a skill tree for the creeps you can buy, so the pool you send from is a consequence of what you built" — one coherent identity per run rather than two shopping trips. It is the only scheme here that makes the upgrade graph pay for itself twice.

It is also the one piece of direction the research pushed back on. Exactly one shipped precedent was found — a Bloons TD Battles 1 upgrade that let you send a tier earlier, gating the schedule rather than the pool; it shipped with a carve-out and the sequel removed it. Every other game surveyed gates the opposite way: constrain the defense, keep the attacking vocabulary universal, because a send is only a read if both players already know the whole menu.

Two named failure modes bite hardest here. Double-dominance under one purse — one build wins both halves and there is no second wallet to separate them. And counter-picking collapsing to a lookup, since in the lobby you already see the opponent's defense at the end of each round. Unexplored rather than known-bad, and it is not a decision the upgrade scheme has to make on its own.

What KayKit gives you

The best fit of anything here, and by accident. Adventurers and Skeletons were built as deliberate mirrors — Knight/Warrior, Rogue/Rogue, Mage/Mage — so a tower line and the creep line it would unlock already exist as matched silhouettes with a shared rig. The identity claim would be visible on the board without a single new model.

The reading that matters

Asset reality check

What the packs can and cannot do

Five facts about the bundle do most of the work in the grading above, and one of them is a genuine gap.

  • One rig, 133 clips, all fifty-seven characters. A class change is a mesh swap with no animation cost. This is why FORK and FUSION are cheap and would be expensive in almost any other pack.
  • One 1024×1024 gradient atlas. A recolour is one small PNG, so any scheme whose visual state is a palette is effectively free — which is every vertical scheme, and the element half of RECIPE.
  • Attachment points are how the pack is built. 25+ swappable weapons and accessories, removable helmets, four swappable face plates on the Action Figure, authored muzzle-flash meshes. This is what lifts the art veto on CROSSPATH and makes KIT nearly free.
  • Three characters ship as transformation pairs — Werewolf, Animatronic, 4-GTN. STANCE exists as art whether or not it exists as a rule.
  • The tower-plausible half of the roster is about twenty-five models, not fifty-seven. Adventurers is all eight; the Mystery Monthly series contribute maybe seventeen more that read as defenders rather than as threats. That is comfortably enough for five bases and twelve leaves — and it is not enough for a fifty-six-tower combinatorial roster made of characters, which is the sharpest asset-side argument against RECIPE in its full form.
The gap, stated honestly

Flying is thin. The Avian Swordsman and the Witch on her broom, and that is the list. The wave-9 anchor is currently written as the flying-units case, so if it stays that way, either the answer comes from Quaternius Ultimate Monsters (free, CC0, 50 animated) — already the named supplement — or the anchor's axis changes. There are also no true beasts and no insects in KayKit.

The schemes compose

THE ARCHETYPE
STACK		 FORK + TECH + HEIGHT + STANCE Five recognisable classes, each forking once into one of two or three named leaves. Ranks are bought per type, so the board changes in visible steps. Height is the cheap filler decision, and a free stance toggle covers a mis-read anchor. Twelve to fifteen authored towers with real numbers. Depth lives between towers, so no single panel shows a player their whole decision.
THE LATTICE
STACK		 FUSION + MOSAIC + KIT + HEIGHT Five bases; what you can fuse into depends on what you placed beside what. Placement and identity are one decision, on a maze that changes every rotation. A weapon slot gives a late, reversible correction. Ten dual classes plus five bases, and adjacency must be drawn or it cannot be read. The fixed-point check on fusion is mandatory before anything ships.
THE DENSE
STACK		 CROSSPATH + DRAFT + APEX Six towers, twenty-five configurations each. Upgrade cards arrive in the shared public offering, so what you can build is metered and everyone saw the menu. One apex per run gives the last three rounds a target. The most authored upgrades of the three, and depth that is a private optimisation in a game whose signature is scouting a composition.

What I need from you

Four decisions, in order
  • Does the defense get a pick axis, or is it gold only? Everything downstream turns on this. Gold-only makes the upgrade graph a spending order; DRAFT makes it a metered commitment and costs only interface work. This is the biggest single decision on the page.
  • Per instance or per type? A ten-round run and a board of a dozen towers argues hard for per type, and per type is also the cheapest thing for the sweep. It is a big change in feel and I would not assume it.
  • Do you want named archetypes as leaves, or abstract configurations? This is really a question about what the game looks like — a board of recognisable classes, or a board of the same tower differently equipped. The assets serve both; the accessibility argument favours the first.
  • Is wave 9's anchor still the flying case? If so, the air answer has to come from outside KayKit, and that is worth knowing before the bundle is bought.
